## The problem

Furniture is high-consideration and visually driven. Customers need to browse by room, style and
dimensions, and they abandon the purchase if the product page makes them guess how something fits.
The brief was a storefront where browsing and filtering feel effortless, and where a customer can
complete an order without phoning the shop.

## What I built

A Django storefront where the catalogue is filterable, the product pages answer sizing questions
before they are asked, and checkout is self-service.

**Product catalogue** — furniture organised by category with filters on the attributes customers
actually search by, plus detailed product pages carrying dimensions and materials.

**Shopping cart** — add to cart, adjust quantities and live price calculation, with the cart
persisting across sessions and devices.

**Order management** — complete checkout with order tracking and full order history, so customers
can revisit a purchase without contacting support.

**User accounts** — registration, login and profile management, which is what makes order history
possible in the first place.

**Admin dashboard** — products, categories, inventory and orders managed from a backend panel
rather than a database console.

**Responsive design** — a mobile-first interface across catalogue, product page and checkout,
because furniture browsing happens on phones as often as on desktops.

## Tech stack

| Layer | Choice | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Backend | Django, Python | Strong admin tooling out of the box and a reliable ORM layer for the business rules behind pricing and stock |
| Database | MongoDB | Product documents carry wide, frequently changing attribute sets; storing them as documents avoided constant schema migration |
| Front-end | Bootstrap, JavaScript | Consistent responsive components across a large catalogue without a heavy custom design system |

## Challenges and learnings

The defining challenge was running Django against MongoDB while keeping orders and inventory
consistent. Document storage makes product variety easy and transactional guarantees hard, and
inventory is exactly the case that needs them — two customers cannot buy the last chair.

The resolution was to keep products in MongoDB, where they genuinely benefit, and move order and
inventory data to a transactional store so stock decrements happen atomically. Accepting two data
stores was less elegant than one, and considerably more correct.

Real-time cart updates were the second problem. Naively reloading totals on every interaction made
the interface feel slow and lost state under rapid input. Calculating totals server-side on
authoritative state, rather than trusting client-side arithmetic, made the cart both fast and
correct.

Performance tuning mattered at catalogue scale. Filtering without indexes degraded as the range
grew, so the frequently filtered attributes were indexed and the category page constrained its
queries deliberately rather than returning the whole collection and filtering in the view.

## Outcome

Delivered in **2024**. The business sells online end to end, customers can filter and order
without contacting the shop, and the catalogue can grow without schema changes for every new
furniture attribute.
